The Monster Card Game, is a TCG where you command an army of monsters to defeat your opponent. This is the second iteration of the game, hence its title, MCG II. (Or the MCG TCG)
SET UP:
 Each player has a deck of X cards. Select 1 monster card to put on top of your deck. Shuffle the rest of your deck and draw your hand.

THE FIRST TURN:
On your first turn you may not draw any cards or use any abilities, and must play a monster.
The draw step is skipped entirely (you begin in the play step).
Besides that, it follows the same structure as a normal turn.
When playing a monster, place it in the front of a slot to enter active, or in the space behind that slot to enter withdrawn.

Each player takes their turn in a clockwise order.

TAKING YOUR TURN:
You may do any of the following in this order:
	• Draw a card
	• Use any ability available once and/or play one card
	• Withdraw or advance a card   
	• Attach an energy to any card
	End your turn and resolve any effects
	
THE CARDS:
There a three types of card:
A monster, has health, abilities, and other attributes.
An Effect, a one-time card that when played does things like healing monsters, damage, giving energy…
And Equipment, attached to a monster giving it long lasting buffs. Has its own health, and can be destroyed.

Objective: defeat your opponent's monsters until they run out of points. Each player as 6 points, and each monster has a point value. When a monster is slain, its owner loses points equal to the monster's point value. You lose if you have no points.
You also lose if you have no monsters in play.

Monster Cards have an HP. If this runs out, they die and are discarded.
They also have a weakness, attacks of that type do 1.5x damage to them. (Rounded to the nearest 10)
Then there are abilities, which require Energy. Some abilities just require a certain amount of energy to be attached to that card to work, others consume energy in the process and some do both. These abilities often do damage, which they may do to any active card, but it may not be split unless specifically stated.
Monsters occasionally have defense, which lowers an incoming attack by 10 damage for every point, for a minimum of 10 damage.

Withdrawing and advancing: A card may have two states: Active or Withdrawn. Active cards may use abilities, be targeted by enemy attacks, and wreak havoc. Withdrawn cards sit back and relax, they can still be healed, targeted by a select few abilities, and can still receive energy. Their passive abilities also take effect, and if they have any 'ranged' abilities they may use them while withdrawn. After using an ability and/or playing a card, you may withdraw one card, moving it to a withdrawn state, or activate a card, moving it to its active state. Then you may attach an energy to any card.

TERMS
Damage - represented by a sword icon.
Health - represented by a heart icon.

Ranged - Ranged abilities may be used while withdrawn. Ranged attacks may also be used to target withdrawn enemy cards, but only when used from the frontline.
Target - any card you would normally be able to attack, generally a card in the frontlines. If your opponent has no active cards, you may attack their withdrawn cards as if they are active. They still must activate those cards to use their normal (non-ranged) abilities.
Remove - The card is taken from the game area and discarded. Different from slain.
Slain or killed - the card has reached zero or less HP and is removed, but you also lose points equal to the card's value.
Adjacent - the card to the immediate right or left of another card. If there is no card to the right and left, then the adjacent card is the nearest one belonging to the adjacent player.
Piercing - the attack's damage is un effected by defense in every way. Sound attacks automatically have this property.
Protect - a protected card may not be attacked until the protector is dead or has been withdrawn.

The energy costs for each ability are represented by the number of dots, which can be either filled or empty. Empty dots mean that the card needs the energy to use the ability, but filled dots means that the energy is consumed as well.
Abilities can have a mix of filled and empty dots. We'll use 0s to represent empty dots and + to represent filled dots in documents.

ADDITIONAL STATUS RULES
Poison: default 3 turns. At the end of its owner's turn, take 10 damage, or 30 if the card used an ability, advanced, or withdrew that turn.
Wet: default 3 turns. Water attacks against it gain +10 base damage before weakness and defence.
Poison, Wet, Goo, and Frozen refresh rather than stack. Burn may stack. A timed status applied during its target owner's turn does not tick at that same turn's end; otherwise it begins on the target owner's next turn.
Duplicate equipment names may not be attached to the same monster.

An illuminated monster triggers Light weakness in addition to its normal damage type until its controller's next turn. Prism Lens keeps its monster illuminated while attached. Light weakness and its own weakness never both multiply; only one ×1.5 applies. +1/6 adds the lowest die face that was not already a success, to a maximum of 6/6.
